## 2. The three findings that mattered

### 2.1 The objective function never mentioned energy

`fitnessOf` was `record.value / record.ticks`, where `value` was *the stimulus of the task being
performed*. It rewarded **being busy at something the colony wanted**, never delivering anything.
Two call sites scored a creep as productive for walking home and for arriving at a full nest with
nothing to unload. About 7,500 generations had selected under that, with best fitness frozen at
0.9055 — an unbeatable busywork champion no honest creep could displace.

Fitness now counts **energy that actually left the creep**, measured as a decrease in its own
store. One measurement covers transfers, upgrades, construction and repair, and no call site can
misreport it.

**Result: the single largest gain of the session.** Output 1.85 → 4.57 energy/tick at comparable
headcount. Best fitness unfroze; `fitMean` rose 0.332 → 2.089 while gene spreads narrowed, which
is convergence under selection rather than drift.

### 2.2 The colony could not build anything

Only two `createConstructionSite` calls existed in the entire codebase — roads and extensions.
There was no code path that could ever build a storage, container, link or tower, though RCL 5
allowed all four. Total energy capacity was 1,800, so income the colony could not absorb was
simply never harvested; a Screeps source refills on a fixed 300-tick timer whether or not anyone
draws from it.

Added this session: **storage** (granary), a **source→controller link pair**, and **two towers**.

### 2.3 The collapses were NPC invaders, not demography

This is the one that cost the most. Population repeatedly crashed 26 → 1–5. Across roughly eight
iterations I attributed it to spawn policy and shipped: deficit-scaled stagger, pre-spawn
replacement, deficit-scaled growth reserve, lead-time jitter, a full-body requirement, and two
separate `COLONY_MAX` derivations.

Some of those fixed real defects. None addressed the cause.

The cause was **NPC invader cores in W4N7 and W5N7** spawning waves that reached the home room.
Time-of-death instrumentation showed it in a single observation: `diedYoung` went 0 → 27 across a
fall, `medianDeathTTL` jumped 1 → 573, and individual creeps lived **27, 31 and 56 ticks** of a
1500-tick lifespan — the travel time to a neighbouring room and no further. `hostile-room` then
fired 25 times for **W5N8 itself**.

Fixed by detecting `STRUCTURE_INVADER_CORE`, checking danger before novelty when choosing a
foraging room, fleeing hostile rooms, and building towers.

5. Containers (5 allowed) unbuilt. Honeybee cross-inhibition for task oscillation. Pheromone-

## 4. How to run the loop better

**This is the most transferable part of the session.** Every serious error came from measurement,
not from code.

### 4.1 The rule that would have saved eight iterations

> **A snapshot cannot falsify a periodic phenomenon.**

"No hostiles present" was checked repeatedly and was true every time, because invader waves are
periodic and every check landed between them. That false negative anchored eight iterations of
work on the wrong subsystem.

**Measure events, not state.** Time-of-death — an event record — solved in one observation what
state-inspection could not solve in eight iterations. When something bad happens intermittently,
instrument *the moment it happens*, not the conditions around it.

### 4.2 Ask what a metric reads when nothing is wrong

Two metrics were built, trusted, and thrown away:

- `peakDeathsPer100` — the maximum over fifteen integer buckets. At a mean of ~1.3 per bucket the
  max of fifteen Poisson draws is routinely 3 **with no synchronisation at all**. It read "over
  capacity" for perfectly healthy colonies. I confirmed a mechanism on it before checking its
  healthy baseline, which was a base-rate error.
- `worstDeficit` — forward-looking, inspecting the TTLs of creeps *currently alive*. During a
  collapse those are fresh replacements with full lifespans, so survivors looked healthy while the
  old cohort died around them. It read 0 always.

**Establish the healthy baseline before crediting any confirmation.** A signal present in both the
failure case and the healthy case explains nothing.

### 4.3 Thresholds

Three collapses came from thresholds that could never fire, all provable at the keyboard:

- `granary ?? nestSink` — always the granary, because storage free capacity is 1,000,000.
- `0.75 * nestCapacity` — demanded 1350 when the nest never exceeded 950.
- `GROWTH_MIN_RESERVE` gating *replacement* — with a fixed cap, almost every spawn is replacement,
  so a gate written to prevent over-investment throttled maintenance during a die-off.

**Before shipping any threshold: compute the range the quantity actually takes and confirm the
threshold falls inside it. Then ask what it is measured against** — a fraction of total capacity is
almost always the wrong denominator. Prefer something tied to what the thing is *for*
(`NEST_SPAWN_RESERVE = MAX_BODY_UNITS * BODY_UNIT_COST` is the model).

### 4.4 Other rules earned the hard way

- **A metric can move for a reason unrelated to your change.** Birth gaps stopped showing the
  36-tick lock and looked like the jitter had worked — but only because bodies had shrunk to 3
  parts with a 9-tick spawn time.
- **Noise floor is ±1.1 energy/tick** over 6000-tick windows, established from two consecutive
  identical-configuration windows reading 3.91 and 5.02. Report per-creep alongside total; it is
  the more sensitive figure and it caught an over-correction.
- **Filter telemetry by timestamp when a field is new.** `JSONExtractInt` returns 0 for rows
  predating a field, silently filling a baseline with fake zeros.
- **A check reading only built structures fires again while the thing is a construction site.**
  This put two links side by side at the controller.
- **Infrastructure with no filler transports nothing.** The link pair was inert until source links
  became supply targets.

### 4.5 Process changes worth making

1. **Budget one iteration per hypothesis, then force a different class of explanation.** Five
   consecutive spawn-policy fixes should have triggered a category switch far sooner.
2. **Instrument before theorising.** The single most productive change all session was recording
   time-of-death, and it was cheap. Telemetry-only changes were exempted from the one-change rule
   for good reason — lean on that harder and earlier.
3. **Keep the "verdict" column honest.** `unverified` and `falsified` rows in
   `docs/loop-state.md` were more useful later than the `worked` rows.
4. **The loop prompt grew to ~200 lines and that was fine** — the accumulated trap list is what
   stopped repeats. Keep growing it; do not trim it for tidiness.
